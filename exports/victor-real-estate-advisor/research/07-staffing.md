# 7. Staffing

*Research as of November 2026. See [10-sources.md](10-sources.md) for sources.*

## Typical team for a 1,000–1,500 sq ft café (open ~7 AM–6 PM, 7 days)

| Role | Headcount | Pay (Miami estimate) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| General manager / head of coffee | 1 | $50K–$70K salary | Runs schedule, ordering, quality, hiring. Owner may fill this at first. |
| Shift leads | 2 | $18–$22/hr + tips | Open and close; certified food manager |
| Baristas | 6–10 (mix of full- and part-time) | $16–$19/hr + tips | Bilingual strongly preferred |
| Food prep / dishwasher | 1–2 part-time | $15–$17/hr | Fewer if food is bought in |
| Bookkeeper / accountant | Outsourced | $300–$1,000/month | Payroll, sales tax, books |

Peak hours (7–10 AM) usually need 3–4 people on the floor; afternoons 2.

## Wages and labor law (Florida)

- **Minimum wage:** **$15.00/hr since Sept 30, 2026** (final step of the 2020 voter-approved increase). Inflation adjustments follow each year — confirm the next date.
- **Tipped minimum:** $11.98/hr (tip credit $3.02), only if tips bring the worker to at least $15. Many cafés pay the full $15+ and share tips instead.
- **Miami barista pay data** differs by source: about $14.23 median (BLS-based), $16.64–$24.32 for temp shifts (Indeed Flex), and ~$41K/year (~$19.85/hr) typical (Indeed). Plan on **$16–$19/hr plus tips** to compete for good staff.
- **Tip pooling:** allowed among tipped staff; managers and owners may **not** keep employee tips (federal FLSA).
- **Overtime:** time-and-a-half over 40 hours/week (federal).
- **Workers' comp:** required with 4+ employees.
- **E-Verify:** Florida requires private employers with **25 or more employees** to use E-Verify for new hires.
- **Minors:** Florida child-labor rules limit hours for 14–17-year-olds; check before hiring high school students.

## Hiring in Miami

- Bilingual (English/Spanish) staff are a big plus with customers.
- Competition for good baristas is strong — Black Sheep, The Coffee and others are hiring.
- Where to recruit: Indeed, Culinary Agents, local specialty-coffee Instagram community, Miami Dade College and FIU hospitality programs, barista competitions and latte-art throwdowns.

## Training plan

1. **Before opening:** 2–3 weeks paid training — espresso dial-in, milk, menu, POS, cleaning, customer service, food safety.
2. **Certifications:** DBPR-approved food handler training (e.g., SafeStaff) for all staff; Certified Food Protection Manager for managers and shift leads within 30 days.
3. **Coffee skills:** consider Specialty Coffee Association (SCA) barista courses or training from the roaster supplier (many roasters train wholesale customers free).
4. **Ongoing:** weekly cuppings, calibration, and clear recipes/specs.

## Retention ideas

- Pay above the minimum; fair, transparent tip sharing.
- Predictable schedules (posted 2 weeks ahead).
- Free drinks and shift meals; staff discount.
- A path up: barista → shift lead → manager; pay for SCA courses.
- Celebrate staff on social media (also good marketing).

## Scheduling and labor control

- Use scheduling software tied to the POS (e.g., 7shifts, Homebase, Toast) to match staffing to hourly sales.
- Target labor at ~30–35% of sales; track it weekly.
