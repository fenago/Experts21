# Usability review: Experts21 web app

**Reviewer:** Wren (ux-designer-apple-hig) · **Date:** 2026-11-21 · **Build reviewed:** `web/` as of today (`npm run build`, served with `vite preview`)

**Method:** walked 6 tasks in Chromium with Playwright at desktop (1280×860) and phone (`iPhone 13`, 390×664 CSS px) sizes. The API was mocked: normal answers, a single-expert HTTP 500, one expert failing inside a whole-team run, a slow run that I stopped, and a password-free run. Also tested: keyboard only, 200% text (root `font-size: 200%`, and 640×430 at 2× zoom to mimic browser zoom), dark mode, `contrast: more`, `reducedMotion: reduce`, `forcedColors: active`, and axe-core (WCAG 2.0/2.1/2.2 A and AA) in each mode. Screenshots are in my scratchpad, not the repo; each one is described where I cite it. **Not tested:** a real VoiceOver session (I checked the markup with axe and by hand instead), a real phone, and the live Netlify functions. Error wording that comes from the server is quoted from `web/netlify/lib/claude.mjs`.

**Tasks:** (1) ask one expert a question · (2) ask the whole team · (3) find a specific expert (27 in the list) · (4) recover from an error · (5) use it on a phone · (6) do 1–4 with keyboard only, a screen reader, and 200% text.

---

## Bottom line

The core idea works. Picking an expert and getting a streamed, readable answer is quick, and the whole-team flow (Elena's plan, then expert cards, then the combined answer) is easy to follow. **The app is weakest when something goes wrong, and for anyone who doesn't use a mouse at normal text size.** If a single-expert question fails, the question disappears and the user sees an API code. Screen readers announce nothing. On a phone, the expert menu can't be closed with Esc or by tapping outside it. **Fix #1–#3 first.** Together they take about a day and remove the worst failure (losing your work). Next, replace the phone menu with a tab bar and add expert search (#4, #5).

## Quick wins (this week, all S)

1. **Never lose the question** (#1): keep the message, restore the text, add **Try again**, and re-send after unlocking.
2. **Plain-language errors with Try again** (#3): one copy map in `api.js`.
3. **Live status for screen readers** (#2), plus **Skip to question**, `aria-current`, and a focus ring (#10).
4. **Stop is not failure** (#13), and the reduced-motion rule (#15).
5. **One-line expert header with About** (#11), and drop model names from the heading (#14).

## What's working — keep it

- **Streaming answers** in readable Markdown (tables render, `App.jsx:24-30`), with a **Stop** button always in the composer's place while busy.
- **Named, persona-based status**: "Victor is thinking…", "Elena is combining the answers…", "Frank is stress-testing the advice…". This is the right voice (`states-good-vs-bad.md` §2).
- **The whole-team structure**: Elena's plan, then per-expert cards with their brief, then a clearly framed **Combined answer from Elena** (`d2-team-done.png`). It makes a 4–8-call process understandable.
- **Partial answers are kept on Stop or cut-off** in single-expert chat ("*(Incomplete.)*", `App.jsx:191`), and a length-limit note is shown (`App.jsx:186`).
- **Conversations stay separate per expert** while you switch between them in a session.
- **Dark mode passes axe.** Landmarks are right (`nav aria-label="Experts"`, `main`, one `h1`), `lang="en"` is set, and no horizontal scroll at 2× zoom. All desktop targets are ≥ 44px.
- **The disclaimer is always visible**: "General information, not legal, tax, or financial advice. Research dated November 2026."

## Navigation recommendation

This app has three real destinations: **ask the team**, **pick an expert**, and (once #7 ships) **past conversations**. Following the HIG ("Consider using a tab bar first", then "adapting your tab bar into a sidebar automatically"; `knowledge/ux-designer-apple-hig/docs/navigation-tab-bars-and-sidebars-reference.md`), use one information architecture in two presentations, switched by **width, not device**:

| Width | Presentation | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **< 700px (phones)** | **Bottom tab bar, 3 tabs:** **Team** · **Experts** · **History** | Icon + one-word label, ≥ 44px, `aria-current="page"` plus a filled icon on the selected tab. **Experts** is a full-screen searchable list (2-line rows, grouped by area). Tapping an expert pushes their chat with a back button labeled **"Experts"**. The tab bar stays visible, except it may hide while the keyboard is open. No actions in the tab bar: "New conversation" goes in the chat header. Until History exists, ship 2 tabs rather than a disabled third ("Don't disable or hide tab bar buttons"). |
| **700–1023px** | Sidebar as an overlay, opened from a toolbar button | Scrim; Esc and scrim tap close it; focus is trapped inside and returns to the button; `inert` on `main`. |
| **≥ 1024px** | **Persistent sidebar** (keep today's) | Order: search field → **Ask the whole team** (pinned) → **Recent** (last 3–5 conversations) → expert groups. One level of grouping, 2-line rows. Same names and order as the phone tabs and list. Nothing important at the bottom. |

This removes the hamburger-only drawer (#4), puts search where people look first (#5), and makes "Ask the whole team" a top-level destination on every size.

## Open questions for testing with real users

Run with the consultant and Dr. Lee (5 sessions of 20 minutes each is enough). Have them think aloud on a laptop and on their own phone.

1. **Team or one expert?** Given "Can we afford the Brickell space?", which do they pick, and why? Does "Ask the whole team" read as the default?
2. **Finding an expert:** "Find who can tell you what permits you need in Coral Gables." Time it. Do they scroll, search, or give up and ask the team?
3. **Follow-ups:** after a team answer, do they type a follow-up into the team box and expect it to remember (#8)? That tells us whether to build context or just label it.
4. **Reading team answers:** do they open the expert cards, or read only Elena's combined answer? This decides whether the cards should start collapsed with a one-line summary.
5. **Saving:** do they expect answers to be there tomorrow? Is browser-only storage acceptable on the devices they use, or do they need export/email instead? Ask whether anyone else uses the same computer.
6. **Model badges:** does "opus / sonnet / haiku" mean anything to them? Would "deeper / faster" or a cost hint help them choose, or is it noise?
7. **Waiting:** how long will they wait for a team answer before thinking it froze? Does "1 of 3 done" change that?
