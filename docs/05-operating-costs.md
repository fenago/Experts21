# 5. Operating Costs and Break-Even

*Research as of November 2026. See [10-sources.md](10-sources.md) for sources. The model below uses our own illustrative assumptions — the finance agent should rebuild it with real quotes.*

## Industry benchmarks (share of sales)

| Cost | Benchmark | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cost of goods (COGS) | **25–32%** overall | Drinks 22–25%; bought-in pastries 35–45% |
| Labor (incl. payroll taxes) | **~30%** target | Hard to hit in Miami at $15 minimum wage unless sales are strong |
| Rent (incl. NNN charges) | **≤ 10%** | Miami broker rule of thumb |
| Marketing | **3–6%** | |
| Net profit | **2.5%–15%**, well-run 15–20% | Sources disagree widely; 13.8% average reported in a 2025 independent shop survey |

## Miami-specific cost drivers

### Rent (per sq ft per year, asking)

| Area | Asking rent | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Miami-Dade average (Q3 2026) | ~$50 | Cushman & Wakefield |
| Coral Gables | ~$52 (3Q24, older data) | Matthews |
| Brickell | ~$68 (avg, Oct 2025), vacancy ~2.2% | Miami Today |
| Brickell & Miami Beach | $70+ | Marcus & Millichap |
| Wynwood | high $60s–low $80s average; **$100+** on prime blocks | CoStar via The Real Deal |

Most Miami retail leases are **NNN** (tenant also pays property tax, insurance, and common-area costs, often another $15–$30/sq ft). Example: 1,200 sq ft at $60 base + $18 NNN = **~$7,800/month**.

### Labor

- Florida minimum wage: **$15.00/hr since Sept 30, 2026**; tipped minimum $11.98 (tip credit $3.02). Annual inflation adjustments follow (confirm the next effective date).
- Barista pay in Miami: roughly **$14–$20/hr** depending on source; Indeed shows ~$19.85/hr typical. Expect to pay **$16–$19/hr + tips** to attract good baristas.
- Add ~10–15% for payroll taxes (FICA, federal and Florida reemployment tax) and workers' comp.

### Other Miami factors

- **Electricity:** air conditioning runs nearly all year; budget more than national averages.
- **Insurance:** Florida commercial property premiums rose ~20–30% above national averages in 2025; hurricane deductibles are often 2–15% of insured value; flood is a separate policy.
- **Sales tax:** 7% in Miami-Dade (6% state + 1% county surtax) — collected from customers, not a cost, but must be filed.
- **No state personal income tax** in Florida (helps owner take-home).

## Illustrative monthly P&L — 1,200 sq ft café

Assumptions: 340 transactions/day, $8.25 average ticket, 30 days.

| Line | Monthly | % of sales |
|---|---|---|
| **Sales** | **$84,000** | 100% |
| COGS (coffee, milk, food, cups) | $25,200 | 30% |
| Card processing (~3%) | $2,500 | 3% |
| **Gross profit** | **$56,300** | 67% |
| Hourly labor (~50 hrs/day × $17) | $25,500 | 30% |
| Manager salary | $5,500 | 7% |
| Payroll taxes & workers' comp (~12%) | $3,700 | 4% |
| Rent + NNN | $7,800 | 9% |
| Utilities (power, water, internet) | $2,500 | 3% |
| Insurance | $1,200 | 1% |
| Marketing | $2,500 | 3% |
| Repairs, maintenance, cleaning | $1,200 | 1% |
| Software, accounting, licenses, misc. | $1,500 | 2% |
| **Operating profit (before loan & owner pay)** | **~$4,900** | **~6%** |
| Loan payment (e.g., $400K, 10 yr, ~10%) | ~$5,300 | 6% |
| **Cash left after debt** | **~ -$400** | ~0% |

**Reading the model:** at ~340 transactions/day, the shop roughly breaks even after debt service. Every extra 50 transactions/day (~$12K/month in sales) adds about **$8K/month** to profit, because most costs are fixed. Volume and average ticket matter enormously.

## Break-even formula

```
Break-even monthly sales = Fixed monthly costs ÷ Contribution margin
Fixed costs  ≈ $56,700 (labor $34,700, rent $7,800, other $8,900, loan $5,300)
Contribution ≈ 67% (100% − 30% COGS − 3% card fees)
Break-even   ≈ $84,600/month ≈ $2,820/day ≈ 340 transactions at $8.25
```

## Levers to improve the numbers

1. **Raise average ticket:** food attach, size upgrades, signature drinks, retail beans and merch.
2. **Smaller space / lower rent:** a 600–900 sq ft site with a strong walk-up window.
3. **Labor scheduling:** tight staffing in slow afternoon hours; cross-training.
4. **Menu mix:** cold brew, matcha and specialty drinks carry better margins than plain drip.
5. **Wholesale and catering:** office coffee, events, and selling beans add revenue without more rent.
6. **Negotiate the lease:** free-rent months, TI allowance, rent tied partly to sales (percentage rent).
