## Miami coffee culture

Miami has two coffee cultures side by side:

1. **Cuban / Latin coffee.** Cafecito (sweet Cuban espresso), colada (shared cafecito), cortadito and café con leche, sold all day from *ventanitas* (walk-up windows) at bakeries and restaurants. Prices are low (often about $1.50–$3). This is a daily habit for a large share of residents. It is also strong competition on price and speed.
2. **Third-wave specialty coffee.** Single-origin espresso, pour-over, local roasting. Panther Coffee started this in Miami in 2010 and has been ranked among the world's top coffee shops. The scene keeps growing, with independents, roasters and now international brands.

The opportunity many newer shops chase is the **gap between the two**: specialty-grade beans and craft, served with Miami's Latin flavors, speed and social energy.

## Trends shaping 2026–2027

- **New entrants.** Black Sheep Coffee (UK) opened in Coconut Grove in Aug 2025, plans Doral and Miami Beach, and signed a 6+ unit Miami Beach franchise deal in May 2026. Brazil's **The Coffee** opened its first U.S. shop in Wynwood in Dec 2025. Drive-thru chains **7 Brew** and **Lola Beans** (5-unit deal, Sept 2026) are coming to South Florida.
- **Non-coffee drinks.** Matcha, cold brew, and sweet iced signature drinks drive much of the younger customer's spending. The official Miami tourism site now lists "best coffee and matcha shops" together.
- **Small formats.** Kiosks, trailers, and walk-up windows are growing because they need less capital and less rent. One recent Miami independent started as a trailer in Feb 2025 before opening a storefront.
- **Coffee prices.** Green coffee hit record highs in 2025. U.S. tariffs on Brazilian coffee were removed in Nov 2025, and a record Brazil crop is forecast for 2026/27, but prices were still described as high in late 2026. Roasters pass changes on with a 4–6 month lag.
- **Restaurant shake-out.** CoStar and WLRN (Nov 2026) describe Miami's post-pandemic restaurant boom as overbuilt, with rent-driven closures and a weak summer ("ghost summer").

## Seasonality

- **Peak:** roughly November–April (tourist and snowbird season, Art Basel in early December, Miami Music Week/Ultra in March).
- **Slow:** summer and early fall. Heat cuts walk-in traffic and hurricane season runs **June 1–November 30**.
- Plan cash flow for 4–5 slower months each year.

## What this means for us

- Demand exists, but the shop must be clearly different. "Good coffee" alone will not stand out.
- The location and lease will decide more than the menu does.
- A strong iced, non-coffee and food menu matters in Miami's climate.
- Consider a smaller format first to test the concept at lower risk.
