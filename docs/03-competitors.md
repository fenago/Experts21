# 3. Competitors

*Research as of November 2026. See [10-sources.md](10-sources.md) for sources. This is a starting map, not a full count. The site-selection agent should do a block-by-block check around each candidate site.*

## Competitive tiers

### A. Local specialty roasters and cafés (most direct competitors)

| Name | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Panther Coffee** | Miami's first small-batch specialty roaster (2010). Wynwood flagship plus several shops; ranked No. 30 on a World's 100 Best Coffee Shops list. Strong wholesale presence. **Closing its Sunset Harbour (Miami Beach) shop Feb 28, 2026 due to rent.** |
| **Per'La Specialty Roasters** | Roaster with a café in Coral Gables; its beans are served across the city (e.g., at White Rose). Possible **supplier** as well as competitor. |
| **Pasión del Cielo** | Customers choose their own Latin American single-origin bean, ground to order. Midtown, Brickell, Coral Gables. Closest to a "Latin specialty" positioning. |
| **Vice City Bean, Tinta y Café, Emissary** | Recurring names in "best coffee in Miami" lists. |
| **Suite Habana Cafe** | Left its tiny Wynwood spot after 8 years; reopening (June 2026) inside a Miami Beach hotel. |

### B. International and national specialty brands entering Miami

| Name | Status |
|---|---|
| **Black Sheep Coffee** (UK) | First Florida shop in Coconut Grove (Aug 2025). Doral and Miami Beach planned; 6+ unit Miami Beach franchise deal (May 2026). Specialty-grade robusta is its hook. |
| **The Coffee** (Brazil) | Japanese-inspired, app-driven, small-footprint. First U.S. shop in Wynwood, Dec 3, 2025. Says the U.S. may become its largest market. |
| **7 Brew** | Drive-thru chain targeting Miami in 2026; hired Franklin Street (Aug 2026) to find South Florida sites. |
| **Lola Beans** | 5-unit development deal for Miami (Sept 2026). |

### C. Mass-market chains

Starbucks and Dunkin are everywhere and own convenience, drive-thru and app-based loyalty. They set the customer's price expectation for a latte. Compete on quality, experience and local identity, not on speed or price alone.

### D. Cuban bakeries and ventanitas

Versailles, La Carreta, and hundreds of neighborhood bakeries and cafeterias sell cafecito, colada and pastelitos at very low prices. They own the morning Latin coffee habit. A specialty shop should **respect** this culture (offer its own version) rather than try to beat them on price.

### E. Other substitutes

Juice/smoothie and matcha bars, bakeries with good espresso, hotel lobbies, coworking spaces with free coffee, and home espresso machines.

## Where the gaps may be

- **Latin specialty done well:** specialty-grade cafecito/cortadito, Latin American single-origins with origin stories told in Spanish and English. Only Pasión del Cielo is clearly in this lane.
- **Neighborhoods outside the hot zones:** Wynwood, Brickell and Miami Beach are crowded and expensive. Areas like Little River, Little Haiti, Allapattah, North Miami, Miami Springs, Westchester or Kendall may be under-served by specialty coffee.
- **Speed + quality format:** walk-up window or small kiosk with specialty beans, for people who want ventanita speed with better coffee.
- **Work-friendly space** in areas with many remote workers but few good seats.

## Positioning map (for the strategy agent)

```
                   Higher price / experience
                             |
          The Coffee   Panther   Black Sheep
                             |
   Global / chain ----------+---------- Local / Latin identity
                             |
     Starbucks  Dunkin       |   Pasión del Cielo
         7 Brew              |        Ventanitas (low price)
                             |
                   Lower price / speed
```

Open space: **local + Latin identity + specialty quality**, at a mid-to-premium price.
