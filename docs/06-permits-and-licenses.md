# 6. Permits and Licenses

*Research as of November 2026. See [10-sources.md](10-sources.md) for sources. Rules differ between the City of Miami, Miami Beach, Coral Gables, other cities, and unincorporated Miami-Dade. Confirm each step with the agency before applying. This is not legal advice.*

## Order of steps (City of Miami example)

### Step 1 — Set up the business (state & federal)

| Item | Agency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Form an LLC or corporation | Florida Division of Corporations (Sunbiz) | Annual report due each year (Jan 1–May 1) |
| Fictitious name (DBA) | Sunbiz | Only if trading under a name different from the legal entity |
| EIN (tax ID) | IRS | Free, online |
| Sales tax and reemployment (unemployment) tax registration | Florida Department of Revenue | Sales tax is 7% in Miami-Dade |
| Workers' compensation insurance | Private insurer | Required in Florida for non-construction employers with **4 or more employees** |

### Step 2 — Site and zoning (before signing the lease, if possible)

- **Zoning check:** confirm a café/restaurant is allowed at the address (City of Miami uses Miami 21 zoning). Ask the landlord for any prior Certificate of Use.
- **Parking and change-of-use:** turning retail into a food use can trigger parking rules and county **impact fees**. Ask the city before signing.

### Step 3 — Build-out permits

| Item | Agency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Building permit (construction, plumbing, electrical, mechanical) | City Building Department (or county for unincorporated areas) | Needs licensed architect/engineer drawings |
| Fire plan review and inspection | City Fire Department | Occupancy load, exits, extinguishers, hood if cooking |
| Grease trap / interceptor | Miami-Dade Water & Sewer and DERM (Fats, Oils & Grease program) | Usually required for food uses |
| Backflow prevention device | Water utility | Common for espresso machines and ice makers |
| Sign permit | City | For exterior signs |
| Certificate of Occupancy / Completion | City Building | Issued after final inspections |

### Step 4 — Operating licenses

| Item | Agency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Public Food Service Establishment license** | Florida DBPR, Division of Hotels and Restaurants | "Seating" license for dine-in or "Non-Seating" for take-out only. Plan review may be required for new builds. Online applications can be approved quickly. |
| **Certificate of Use (CU)** | City of Miami (or county in unincorporated areas) | Coffee shops are listed as food service establishments needing a CU. Usually needed before the BTR. |
| **Local Business Tax Receipt — county** | Miami-Dade Tax Collector | Required for every business in the county. Restaurants must show their state license. |
| **Local Business Tax Receipt — city** | City where the shop is located | Needed in addition to the county BTR. Some cities won't issue it without the state restaurant license attached. |

### Step 5 — Optional / situational

| Item | Agency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sidewalk café permit | City of Miami Resilience & Public Works (iPW portal) | $175 application + $10 inspection (current city page); annual per-sq-ft fee may apply. Needs CU, furniture photos, scaled layout. Miami Beach and Coral Gables have their own programs. |
| Alcohol license | DBPR Division of Alcoholic Beverages & Tobacco | Only if selling beer/wine (e.g., espresso martinis). Adds cost and rules. |
| Music license | ASCAP, BMI, SESAC (or a licensed music service) | For playing music in the shop |
| Mobile food license | DBPR | For a cart, trailer or truck; requires a commissary |

## Staff food-safety requirements

- **Certified Food Protection Manager:** food managers must complete training and pass an approved exam **within 30 days of employment**. Plan for more than one certified manager.
- **Food handler training:** all food service employees must complete a DBPR-approved program (such as SafeStaff, available in English and Spanish); renew every 3 years. One vendor states it must be done within 60 days of hire — confirm with DBPR.

## Ongoing compliance

- DBPR inspections (unannounced) — keep temperature logs, sanitation and allergen practices tight.
- Renew DBPR license, county and city BTRs every year.
- Monthly or quarterly sales tax filing; quarterly reemployment tax.
- Post required signs (licenses, labor law posters, hand-washing, choking first aid if seating, etc.).

## Typical delays to plan for

Miami permitting can take **2–4+ months** for plan review and inspections. Hiring an expediter or an architect who knows the local building department can save time. Do not start paying full rent until permits are in hand — negotiate a rent start tied to permit approval or opening.
