# 9. Biggest Risks

*Research as of November 2026. See [10-sources.md](10-sources.md) for sources. Ranked by likely impact on a new Miami café.*

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Why it matters in Miami |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **High rent and bad lease terms** | High | Very high | Brickell/Miami Beach $70+/sq ft, Wynwood $100+ on prime blocks, plus NNN. Panther Coffee is closing a 10+-year shop over rent; Lokal and Mr Chow closed after big rent jumps. |
| 2 | **Underfunding / running out of cash** | High | Very high | Build-outs and permits run over; the first 6–12 months are slow; summer slump. |
| 3 | **Labor cost and staffing** | High | High | $15 minimum wage (Sept 2026) with yearly inflation increases; competition for baristas from new chains. |
| 4 | **Crowded market** | High | High | Local roasters, Starbucks/Dunkin, ventanitas, and new entrants (Black Sheep, The Coffee, 7 Brew, Lola Beans). |
| 5 | **Hurricanes, flooding and insurance** | Medium | Very high | Season runs June 1–Nov 30. Forced closures, power loss, damage. Insurance premiums up sharply; wind deductibles 2–15%; flood excluded from standard policies. |
| 6 | **Coffee price swings** | Medium | Medium | Record prices in 2025; tariffs on Brazil removed Nov 2025 but prices still high in late 2026. Roasters pass changes on 4–6 months later. |
| 7 | **Seasonality and heat** | High | Medium | Tourist season Nov–Apr; "ghost summer" slowdown; heat cuts walk-in traffic. |
| 8 | **Permitting and construction delays** | Medium | High | 2–4+ months of plan review; each month of delay costs rent. |
| 9 | **Location mistakes** | Medium | Very high | Parking, foot traffic that doesn't match the morning rush, construction around the site, flood-prone streets. |
| 10 | **Owner burnout / key-person risk** | Medium | High | Long hours, 7-day operations; if one person holds all knowledge, the business is fragile. |

## How to reduce each risk

1. **Rent and lease**
   - Keep total occupancy cost ≤ 10% of realistic sales.
   - Negotiate free-rent months, a TI allowance, rent start at opening (not lease signing), renewal options, a cap on NNN increases, and a limited personal guarantee.
   - Use a tenant-rep broker and a real-estate lawyer.
   - Consider lower-rent neighborhoods or a smaller footprint with a walk-up window.
2. **Underfunding**
   - Hold a 4–6 month reserve, separate from the build budget.
   - Add a 15–20% contingency to build-out.
   - Test the concept first with a cart, pop-up or trailer.
3. **Labor**
   - Simple menu and efficient bar layout so fewer people can serve more customers.
   - Order-ahead to smooth peaks; data-driven scheduling.
   - Pay a little above market to cut turnover (turnover is expensive).
4. **Competition**
   - A clear, different concept (see [03-competitors.md](03-competitors.md)); strong community and loyalty program.
   - Avoid opening next to a direct specialty competitor.
5. **Hurricanes and insurance**
   - Business owner's policy with **business interruption**, separate **flood** policy, and check the wind deductible.
   - Hurricane plan: shutters, generator hookup or backup for refrigeration, inventory protection, staff communication plan, data backups.
   - Pick a site outside the worst flood zones (check FEMA flood maps); avoid ground-level spaces that flooded before.
6. **Coffee prices**
   - Fixed-price contracts with the roaster for 6–12 months.
   - Price menu with room to absorb changes; review prices twice a year.
7. **Seasonality**
   - Build summer promotions for locals; wholesale, catering and retail beans for steady income.
   - Plan cash flow with lower summer sales.
8. **Permits**
   - Do zoning and CU checks before signing; hire an architect/expediter with local experience; tie rent start to permit approval.
9. **Location**
   - Count foot traffic at 7–10 AM on several days; check parking, visibility, flood history, nearby construction plans.
10. **Burnout**
   - Hire a strong manager early; write down recipes, opening/closing checklists, and supplier contacts.

## Warning signs once open

- Fewer than ~250 transactions/day after 3 months (for a mid-size café)
- Labor above 40% of sales for more than a month
- Cash reserve below 2 months of fixed costs
- Google rating below 4.3 or falling
